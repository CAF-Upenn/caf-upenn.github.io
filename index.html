Patrick Behrer

Patrick Behrer is an Economist in the Development Economics Research Group at The World Bank. His research focuses on the economic causes and consequences of air pollution and climate change around the world. He has a particular focus on how to encourage adaptation to climate change and air pollution exposure. He holds a PhD in Public Policy from Harvard University. Prior to joining The World Bank he was a post doc at Stanford's Center on Food Security and the Environment and a Fulbright Fellow in New Zealand.

Paul Stainier

Paul Stainier is an applied microeconomist and Postdoctoral Fellow in the School of Social Policy and Practice at the University of Pennsylvania. His research covers topics in environmental and labor economics, including the welfare costs of extreme weather events, the barriers to climate adaptation, and the determinants of worker compensation. He holds a PhD in Environment and Sustainability from the University of California, Los Angeles. His personal webpage is paulstainier.com

Husel Husile

Husel is a PhD candidate at the University of Pennsylvania School of Social Policy & Practice. His research focuses on climate adaptation and social resilience in the Global South. He studies how environmental shocks influence social cohesion and social welfare by using large scale panel data, demographic surveys, and satellite-based climate indicators.

Abishek is a first year doctoral student in the School of Social Policy and Practice. Nazar Khalid

Nazar is a Ph.D. candidate in Demography and Sociology at the University of Pennsylvania. His research examines how climatic shocks and large-scale development policies interact with entrenched hierarchies of caste, class, religion, and gender to shape children's education and health in rural India and South Asia. He uses demographic methods, geospatial analysis, and survey-satellite data linkage alongside qualitative fieldwork in flood- and heat-affected communities. He was awarded the Penn Presidential Fellowship (2021–2024) and the Judith Rodin Fellowship (2020–2021). His work has been published in RSF, Economics of Education Review, World Development, and other journals.

Rebecca Pepe

Rebecca is a PhD Candidate in the School of Social Policy and Practice. Her research explores how housing insecurity and the food water energy nexus shape health and well being. As a Climate Adaptation Fellow, Rebecca examines the impact of extreme weather events and the role of forecasting on mitigation efforts among incarcerated populations. Her dissertation focuses on the health effects of water utility disconnections and how to reduce administrative burdens to increase access to water affordability programs. She holds an MPH from the Yale School of Public Health, and a BA in Psychology and BS in Biological Anthropology from the State University of New York at Binghamton.

Sebastian Prandoni

Yabo Vidogbena

Yabo is a development economist interested in human capital formation and structural change in developing countries. In her recent research she investigated how climate change affects human capital formation, specifically how heat impacts the learning and executive functioning of children, and the role of trade in structural change in Sub Saharan African countries. She is a Postdoctoral Fellow at the Population Studies Center and an instructor at the Economics department of the University of Pennsylvania. She obtained her Ph.D. in Economics from the University of Houston in May 2025.

Yujie Zhang

Yujie Zhang is a Postdoctoral Research Fellow at the Population Studies Center at the University of Pennsylvania. She received her Ph.D. in Economics from the University of Houston in summer 2025. Her research interests lie in the fields of Development Economics, using Applied Microeconomics to explore the intersections between climate change, human capital, and social inequality through transnational comparative studies and large-scale data analysis. Her recent paper investigates the long-term effects of natural disasters on education, health, and labor outcomes, highlighting disparities by gender and socio-economic status. She also works with the Asian Development Bank on heat stress, pollution risk, and social welfare in low- and middle-income countries.
